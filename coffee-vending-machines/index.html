Coffee machines, without a published cups-per-day list

People comparing coffee machines usually start with a model and a cups-per-day figure. No coffee model on this site is published with a daily cup rating. The opening below is the format, then the equipment family that is already named.

Hot-drinks cabinet

A coffee vending machine is a self-service cabinet. The menu depends on the equipment and the ingredient system, agreed for that machine rather than published as one list. See coffee vending machines across the UK for the same format outside Northern Ireland.

Bean-to-cup office coffee

A bean-to-cup machine grinds beans for a team in a staff room or reception. No brand or model is named for that format. Rental, lease and fully managed routes are set out on office coffee machines.

SandenVendo equipment

VendingNI supplies SandenVendo equipment. Size and configuration follow the space, the expected usage and the products. No cups-per-day rating is published for it. It is the named vending equipment on this site, chosen at assessment rather than from a cup-capacity table.

How you get a coffee machine

VendingNI’s route is a fully managed placement for a qualifying site. You do not buy the cabinet, and you do not run a daily ingredient round.

  • The machine is supplied and installed once a placement is agreed
  • The first fill, later replenishment and product changes are handled for the site
  • Routine servicing, maintenance and technical support sit with the managed service
  • Card, contactless, Apple Pay and Google Pay depend on the machine and the reader

Hire and purchase are different arrangements, and neither is sold here from a price list. Read how a free managed machine is assessed, or hire and rental in Northern Ireland if that was the search. For vending machines Northern Ireland, enquiries include Belfast, Antrim, Lisburn, Dungannon, Mallusk and other locations. Republic of Ireland coverage is described on the Ireland pages.

Who coffee vending suits, by site size

Hot-drink demand is judged on people in the building, expected cups and whether everyone breaks at once. Expected cups are something you tell us. They are not a catalogue rating.

Below the usual workplace guideline

About 40 regular workplace users is a starting point, not a rule. A smaller site can still be assessed when footfall, long opening hours or shift demand creates steady use. Hybrid attendance means the number of people actually in the building matters more than the payroll.

Around 40 regular users

That is the typical workplace guideline used across this site. It is an assessment starting point. Space, delivery access, products and break patterns are still part of the decision.

Larger workplaces

An office of about 80 people is used on the machines page as a selection example, not a promise of acceptance. A combination machine may cover snacks and chilled drinks in one break area, with coffee assessed separately if hot drinks are the real demand.

The same machine can sit beside snack vending and cold drinks. If one footprint has to carry packaged snacks and chilled drinks, compare a combination machine. Water, power and a place to replenish ingredients without blocking a route are part of the assessment. The practical benefits of workplace coffee are covered in the workplace coffee guide.

Questions people ask

What does it cost?

There is no tariff on this page. For a qualifying location, a fully managed placement means no equipment purchase and no installation charge, and the machine remains part of the service. That result is not guaranteed for every enquiry. The hire cost guide and the cost comparison describe the shape of the cost — the machine, the stock and the staff time — without a price list.

Which coffee setup should we ask for?

A hot-drinks cabinet suits a larger self-service break. Bean-to-cup suits a team that wants coffee from beans in a smaller room. Say how many people are in on a normal day, whether drinks bunch into one break, and whether water and power are where the machine would stand. Office and workplace vending covers the wider refreshment setup.

What does servicing include?

On a machine in the managed service, servicing can include installation, replenishment, product changes, routine maintenance and technical support after a fault is reported through the agreed contact. Refill timing follows usage, capacity, opening hours and access. No response time is published. Where monitoring is fitted, it can show stock, sales or machine status. It is not on every machine. Cleaning requirements are agreed for the chosen setup.

Tell us about the hot-drink demand

Email the site, roughly how many people are in on a normal day, and whether you already know the expected cups, the peak break, and the water and power position.
