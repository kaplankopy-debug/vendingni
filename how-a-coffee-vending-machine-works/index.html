How a Coffee Vending Machine Works

A coffee vending machine heats water, then brews freshly ground coffee or mixes a measured dose of powder, and dispenses the drink for someone to collect. Bean-to-cup machines grind whole beans for each drink, and instant machines mix powder, such as coffee, chocolate or milk, with hot water. The parts inside, and the drinks on the menu, depend on the machine.

What happens inside a coffee vending machine

Water comes from a plumbed supply or from a tank someone fills, depending on the machine, and is heated in a boiler or heater.

A bean-to-cup machine grinds whole beans for each drink and brews them. An instant, or soluble, machine mixes measured powder (coffee, chocolate, milk) with hot water in a mixing bowl. Many machines can also offer tea or hot chocolate. Which drinks are on offer is agreed for that equipment.

Cup machines drop a cup before the drink is poured. Some setups suit people bringing their own mug. Whether a cup is dropped, or a mug is expected, depends on the machine. The snack, drinks, combination and coffee formats are set out on the machines page.

Bean-to-cup and instant: the difference

Bean-to-cup suits sites that want freshly ground coffee. Instant or soluble is simpler and quicker: powder is measured and mixed, and there are no whole beans to refill. The choice is made with the site, from the room, the break and who will drink there, and it depends on the machine that is agreed.

A staff room or reception often starts as a bean-to-cup question. A larger self-service break, or a shift change where people serve themselves, often starts as a hot-drinks cabinet. That choice, including rental and lease, is covered in how to choose an office coffee machine. A workplace in Northern Ireland is assessed on the same basis as a workplace in England, Scotland or Wales.

How to use a coffee vending machine, step by step

The labels and the options depend on the machine. The sequence itself is short.

  1. Choose the drink.
  2. Adjust the options where they are offered, such as strength, milk or sugar.
  3. Pay, or take the drink on free-vend, as agreed for the site.
  4. Wait while the drink is dispensed.
  5. Collect it.

If the machine drops a cup, take that cup. If it is set up for people bringing a mug, use the mug the site expects.

How people pay

How people pay is agreed when the machine is installed. Card, contactless, Apple Pay and Google Pay depend on the reader, the machine, connectivity and the installation. Free-vend is possible where the site agrees it, so someone can take a drink without paying at the cabinet. Cash may be available on some equipment.

Say which you want when you enquire. Cashless arrangements for workplaces across the UK are set out on cashless vending machines.

Cleaning, refilling and servicing

A coffee machine needs routine cleaning, and it needs beans, other ingredients and cups refilled. Who does that work depends on the arrangement.

On a managed machine, replenishment, routine maintenance and technical support are part of the service after a fault is reported through the agreed contact. Where monitoring is fitted, it can show stock, sales or machine status; we confirm whether it is fitted when the machine is agreed.

Rental and lease are separate from the managed service. Who refills and who cleans is then part of what is agreed for the site and the machine.

What your site needs

The site needs floor or counter space, a mains socket, water for a plumbed machine, and access so someone can replenish it. A tank machine still needs room to fill the tank, and a position that does not block a walkway.

Say whether a socket is where the machine would stand, and whether water is nearby. Do not assume a machine can share a socket with a kettle and a microwave. The same points are assessed for a workplace in Northern Ireland and for a workplace elsewhere in the UK. The fuller list is what your workplace needs to host a vending machine.

Getting a coffee vending machine for your workplace

A qualifying workplace can take a coffee vending machine as a fully managed placement. There is no equipment purchase and no installation charge, and the machine remains part of the service. About 40 regular users is a typical starting guideline, subject to assessment. A smaller site can still be assessed when footfall, long opening hours or shift demand creates steady use. Every site is assessed before a machine is placed. How that assessment works is set out in how to get a free vending machine.

Office coffee machines can also be rented or leased. Rental and lease terms depend on the site and the machine. Coverage is the same in England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. The UK service for this cabinet is the coffee vending machine and hot drinks machine page. The same coffee service for workplaces in Northern Ireland is coffee vending machines in Northern Ireland.

Email the workplace, how many people are in on a normal day, whether you want bean-to-cup or an instant cabinet, and how people should pay.
