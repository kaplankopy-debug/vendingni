Vending machine servicing

Vending Machine Repairs, Servicing & Support

Routine servicing and fault support for machines in a VendingNI managed placement. Existing equipment can be discussed, without a published call-out tariff.

Which machines this servicing refers to

Repairs depend on the machine. The named equipment on this site is SandenVendo, including the G-Snack snack and combination example. No fault-rate or response-time figure is published.

SandenVendo equipment

VendingNI supplies SandenVendo equipment. Size and configuration follow the space, the expected usage and the products. No cups-per-day rating is published for it.

Sanden Vendo G-Snack

The Sanden Vendo G-Snack is the named snack and combination example. The overview describes the format. It does not publish a daily vend figure.

Other equipment you already have

If you already have a machine, send the machine type, the location and the issue. We can say whether it is something we can assist with. That is a conversation, not a published repair menu.

How servicing is provided

Routine servicing and machine support are included for machines in the managed vending service on qualifying sites. Faults are reported through the agreed support contact.

  • The machine is supplied and installed once a placement is agreed
  • The first fill, later replenishment and product changes are handled for the site
  • Routine servicing, maintenance and technical support sit with the managed service
  • Card, contactless, Apple Pay and Google Pay depend on the machine and the reader

Faults can involve payment systems, product delivery, refrigeration, configuration or general wear. The response depends on the machine and the problem. No response time is published. A move from an existing machine to a managed arrangement is assessed like any other site. See the managed service.

Who servicing suits, by site size

Servicing for a managed machine follows the same qualification as the placement itself. A repair conversation about equipment you already own is separate, and it still needs the machine type and the location.

Below the usual workplace guideline

About 40 regular workplace users is a starting point, not a rule. A smaller site can still be assessed when footfall, long opening hours or shift demand creates steady use. Hybrid attendance means the number of people actually in the building matters more than the payroll.

Around 40 regular users

That is the typical workplace guideline used across this site. It is an assessment starting point. Space, delivery access, products and break patterns are still part of the decision.

Larger workplaces

An office of about 80 people is used on the machines page as a selection example, not a promise of acceptance. A combination machine may cover snacks and chilled drinks in one break area, with coffee assessed separately if hot drinks are the real demand.

Refill timing on a managed machine follows usage, capacity, opening hours and access. Cleaning requirements are agreed for the setup. They are not a published checklist that applies to every model.

Questions people ask

What does it cost?

There is no tariff on this page. For a qualifying location, a fully managed placement means no equipment purchase and no installation charge, and the machine remains part of the service. That result is not guaranteed for every enquiry. The hire cost guide and the cost comparison describe the shape of the cost — the machine, the stock and the staff time — without a price list. There is no call-out price list. Servicing of machines in the managed service is part of that arrangement for a qualifying site.

What should we send about a fault?

For a managed machine, use the agreed support contact and describe the fault: payment, product delivery, refrigeration, configuration or general wear. For equipment that is not in the managed service, email the machine type, the location and the issue so we can say whether we can assist.

What does servicing include?

On a machine in the managed service, servicing can include installation, replenishment, product changes, routine maintenance and technical support after a fault is reported through the agreed contact. Refill timing follows usage, capacity, opening hours and access. No response time is published. Where monitoring is fitted, it can show stock, sales or machine status. It is not on every machine. Cleaning requirements are agreed for the chosen setup.

Tell us about the machine

Email the location, the machine type if you know it, what it is doing, and roughly how many people use the site.
