Republic of Ireland vending services

Fully Managed Vending Machines in Ireland

Fully managed vending for qualifying workplaces and venues in the Republic of Ireland. The machine is assessed and looked after, rather than sold for the site to run.

Machines for sites in Ireland

Republic of Ireland coverage is supported through the vending partner network. No cups-per-day rating is published. The formats are the same decision as elsewhere: snacks, drinks, combination and coffee.

Snack, drinks, or both

Snack, drinks and combination machines cover packaged food and chilled drinks.

Coffee

Coffee vending is the self-service hot-drinks format. No model name or cups-per-day figure is published.

SandenVendo equipment

VendingNI supplies SandenVendo equipment. Size and configuration follow the space, the expected usage and the products. No cups-per-day rating is published for it. The partner relationship is explained on Vending-Machines.ie.

How sites in Ireland get the service

Qualifying organisations can use a fully managed model: the demand is assessed, a setup is recommended, and the machine is managed after installation. The site does not have to operate a shop.

  • The machine is supplied and installed once a placement is agreed
  • The first fill, later replenishment and product changes are handled for the site
  • Routine servicing, maintenance and technical support sit with the managed service
  • Card, contactless, Apple Pay and Google Pay depend on the machine and the reader

Republic of Ireland service coverage is supported through our vending partner network. Dublin has its own page. Cork, Galway, Limerick and Waterford are named as other commercial areas on this service. They are not separate machine ranges.

Who it suits, by site size

The same size guidelines used for the managed service apply: about 40 regular workplace users as a starting point, about 50 hotel rooms, and about 200 apartments. Each site is still assessed.

Below the usual workplace guideline

About 40 regular workplace users is a starting point, not a rule. A smaller site can still be assessed when footfall, long opening hours or shift demand creates steady use. Hybrid attendance means the number of people actually in the building matters more than the payroll.

Around 40 regular users

That is the typical workplace guideline used across this site. It is an assessment starting point. Space, delivery access, products and break patterns are still part of the decision.

Larger workplaces

An office of about 80 people is used on the machines page as a selection example, not a promise of acceptance. A combination machine may cover snacks and chilled drinks in one break area, with coffee assessed separately if hot drinks are the real demand.

Questions people ask

What does it cost?

There is no tariff on this page. For a qualifying location, a fully managed placement means no equipment purchase and no installation charge, and the machine remains part of the service. That result is not guaranteed for every enquiry. The hire cost guide and the cost comparison describe the shape of the cost — the machine, the stock and the staff time — without a price list.

Which Ireland setup should we ask for?

Say the city, the type of workplace or venue, how many people use it, and whether you need snacks, cold drinks, both, or coffee. Dublin enquiries can start on the Dublin page. The partner page explains the Vending-Machines.ie relationship.

What does servicing include?

On a machine in the managed service, servicing can include installation, replenishment, product changes, routine maintenance and technical support after a fault is reported through the agreed contact. Refill timing follows usage, capacity, opening hours and access. No response time is published. Where monitoring is fitted, it can show stock, sales or machine status. It is not on every machine. Cleaning requirements are agreed for the chosen setup.
