What Your Workplace Needs to Host a Vending Machine

A workplace hosting a vending machine needs floor space where it will stand, a mains socket, and a clear way to deliver and refill it. Snack, chilled drinks and coffee machines run on mains electricity from the site's own supply. Who pays for the machine, and who looks after it, depends on whether the site qualifies for a fully managed placement.

Space and where it stands

The machine needs floor space where it will stand, plus room to open and replenish it. A staff room, a break area, a reception or another suitable shared area is the usual position. People should be able to reach it during the working day without blocking a doorway that has to stay clear.

Delivery access matters on the day it arrives. Doors, stairs or lifts, and the position, decide whether the cabinet can be brought in and set down. Mention a narrow door, stairs with no lift, or a tight corner in the enquiry.

Replenishment access has to work with the building's opening hours. The visit has to reach the machine when the building is open, or at a time the site can arrange. A staff-room machine needs the same things in a smaller space: a shared spot people already use, enough floor for the cabinet, and room to open it.

A site in Northern Ireland is assessed on the same basis as a site in England, Scotland or Wales. Coverage in Northern Ireland is set out on vending machines in Northern Ireland. The same managed service for workplaces across the UK is on the UK vending page.

Does a vending machine need to be plugged in?

Yes. Snack, chilled drinks and coffee machines run on mains electricity. Tell us whether there is a power socket where it would stand. Do not assume a machine can share a socket with a kettle and a microwave. Electrical or site preparation is discussed if required, and it is agreed before delivery.

Water is separate, and it applies to bean-to-cup coffee. Say whether water is nearby. Ask whether the machine needs a plumbed supply or a tank someone fills, and who fills it. Office coffee, including rental and lease, is described on office coffee machines. Snack, drinks and combination formats are set out on the machines page.

Who pays for the electricity?

The machine runs from the site's own supply. How this is handled is part of the conversation when a placement is agreed. Say where the socket is, and what else nearby is already plugged in, so that conversation starts from the real position.

Does it need Wi-Fi or a signal?

Card, contactless, Apple Pay and Google Pay depend on the reader, the machine, connectivity and the installation. The position is checked for that machine, rather than assumed for every site.

Where monitoring is fitted, it can show stock, sales or machine status; we confirm whether it is fitted when the machine is agreed. Cash acceptance may be available on some equipment. Some installations need a data connection for the reader or for monitoring. Others do not. That is confirmed for the machine and the position when cashless vending machines are arranged.

Who pays for the machine?

On a fully managed placement for a qualifying site there is no equipment purchase and no installation charge, and the machine remains part of the service. The workplace provides the floor space. It does not buy the cabinet. About 40 regular users is a typical starting guideline, subject to assessment, and every site is assessed. A smaller site can still be assessed when footfall, long opening hours or shift demand creates steady use.

That is the practical answer to who pays for vending machines, whether a company pays, and whether you have to pay to place one. Office coffee machines can also be rented or leased. Rental and lease apply to office coffee, not to snack and drinks machines, and no terms are set out here.

If the comparison is hire or rental rather than a managed placement, read vending machine hire across the UK and the vending machine hire cost guide. How a qualifying site is supplied, stocked and serviced is explained in how to get a free vending machine. An indicative check before you email is the free vending machine eligibility checker.

What maintenance does it need?

On a managed machine, servicing can include installation, replenishment, product changes, routine maintenance and technical support after a fault is reported through the agreed contact. The host does not normally buy stock or chase faults. Refill timing follows usage, capacity, opening hours and access. The host reports a fault through the agreed contact. This sits inside the fully managed vending service once a placement is agreed.

What to send with your enquiry

A short email is enough to start. Include:

  • the location and town
  • how many people are in on a normal day
  • the opening hours
  • the spot where the machine would stand, and whether that spot has a socket
  • for bean-to-cup, whether water is nearby, and who would fill a tank if the machine is not plumbed
  • delivery access: doors, stairs or lifts, and the position
  • how people will pay
  • the products wanted
  • any site rules

Schools, hospitals and care homes may have their own product rules, and those are part of the brief. Say what the site already allows, and who is expected to use the machine. The same list is used for a workplace in Northern Ireland and for a workplace elsewhere in the UK.
