Residential machines, without a published daily rating

No apartment model is published with a vends-per-day figure. The published guide is the size of the building, then a cashless combination cabinet in a common area.

Combination cabinet

A combination machine can hold packaged snacks and chilled drinks for round-the-clock use. The Sanden Vendo G-Snack is the named example of that format.

SandenVendo equipment

VendingNI supplies SandenVendo equipment. Size and configuration follow the space, the expected usage and the products. No cups-per-day rating is published for it.

Cashless reader

Residents are unlikely to want a coin-only machine. Card and mobile-wallet payments depend on a compatible reader.

How a building gets the service

For a qualifying building the machine, the stock and the routine support are handled for the property team. The building does not open a shop.

  • The machine is supplied and installed once a placement is agreed
  • The first fill, later replenishment and product changes are handled for the site
  • Routine servicing, maintenance and technical support sit with the managed service
  • Card, contactless, Apple Pay and Google Pay depend on the machine and the reader

Stock and refill frequency can follow actual use. Residential placements draw on residential network experience. An apartment enquiry is different from a workplace enquiry: the count is homes, not staff.

Which buildings it suits, by size

About 200 apartments

Approximately 200 apartments is the typical network guideline. It is not a guarantee. Occupancy, secure access, other facilities and likely use are assessed as well.

Larger developments

A building of about 250 units is a selection example on the machines page for a secure common-area combination machine. That example is not an eligibility promise.

Smaller buildings

A smaller block can still be described. Assessment looks at how many people actually live there, whether the common area is used, and whether a delivery can reach it.

Possible positions include reception, a communal lounge and other secure shared spaces, subject to access, power, floor space and the building’s own rules. The position has to be visible and practical to replenish.

Questions people ask

What does it cost?

There is no tariff on this page. For a qualifying location, a fully managed placement means no equipment purchase and no installation charge, and the machine remains part of the service. That result is not guaranteed for every enquiry. The hire cost guide and the cost comparison describe the shape of the cost — the machine, the stock and the staff time — without a price list.

Which apartment setup should we ask for?

The usual request is one cashless combination machine in a secure, well-used common area. A drinks-only or snack-only cabinet is the alternative if one product group dominates. Send the unit count, occupancy, access arrangements and the proposed position.

What does servicing include?

On a machine in the managed service, servicing can include installation, replenishment, product changes, routine maintenance and technical support after a fault is reported through the agreed contact. Refill timing follows usage, capacity, opening hours and access. No response time is published. Where monitoring is fitted, it can show stock, sales or machine status. It is not on every machine. Cleaning requirements are agreed for the chosen setup.

Tell us about the building

Email the location, the number of apartments, how fully they are occupied, how access works, and where a machine could stand.
