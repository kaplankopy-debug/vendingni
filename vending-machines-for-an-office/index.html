Machines for an office or workplace

An office enquiry starts with the format, not a shop of models. The named equipment is SandenVendo. No cups-per-day figure is published, so daily demand is something you describe.

SandenVendo equipment

VendingNI supplies SandenVendo equipment. Size and configuration follow the space, the expected usage and the products. No cups-per-day rating is published for it. The G-Snack overview is the named snack and combination example.

Snacks, drinks, or both

Crisps, chocolate and other snacks sit on a snack machine. Chilled drinks sit on a drinks machine. A combination machine covers both in one break-area position.

Coffee

A self-service hot-drinks cabinet is covered under coffee vending across the UK and coffee vending in Northern Ireland. Bean-to-cup, including rental and lease, is on office coffee machines.

Across the UK, snack, drinks and cashless formats are set out as snack vending machines for UK workplaces, drinks vending machines for UK workplaces and cashless vending machines for UK workplaces.

How an office gets the service

For a qualifying workplace there is no equipment purchase and no installation charge. The machine stays in the managed service. The organisation does not normally run day-to-day stocking.

  • The machine is supplied and installed once a placement is agreed
  • The first fill, later replenishment and product changes are handled for the site
  • Routine servicing, maintenance and technical support sit with the managed service
  • Card, contactless, Apple Pay and Google Pay depend on the machine and the reader

That is the route for an office that wants refreshments without a staffed shop. Hire, rental and purchase leave more of the running with the site, and they are not priced here. Read free managed vending, the managed service, repairs and servicing, and the partner network. Faults go through the agreed contact. No fixed response time applies to every site.

Which workplaces it suits, by size

Offices, hybrid workplaces, factories and other sites with a regular daily population are assessed on who is actually in, not on the name of the building.

Below the usual workplace guideline

About 40 regular workplace users is a starting point, not a rule. A smaller site can still be assessed when footfall, long opening hours or shift demand creates steady use. Hybrid attendance means the number of people actually in the building matters more than the payroll.

Around 40 regular users

That is the typical workplace guideline used across this site. It is an assessment starting point. Space, delivery access, products and break patterns are still part of the decision.

Larger workplaces

An office of about 80 people is used on the machines page as a selection example, not a promise of acceptance. A combination machine may cover snacks and chilled drinks in one break area, with coffee assessed separately if hot drinks are the real demand.

Offices

A break area, reception or staff room where employees and visitors need snacks and drinks. The machine needs to be visible, accessible and practical to replenish.

Hybrid offices

Attendance, break patterns and visitors can make the real daily population smaller than the headcount. A small staff kitchen is relevant when it is the only on-site option.

Other workplaces

Shift sites are assessed by workforce and break timing. See factories and warehouses, gyms, hotels, hospitals and shared working spaces.

Healthier snack and drink choices can be included where they suit the people using the machine. See healthy vending machines. There is no separate range. The service covers England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland, including Belfast, with Republic of Ireland coverage described on the Ireland pages. Every site is assessed on its own floor.

Questions people ask

What does it cost?

There is no tariff on this page. For a qualifying location, a fully managed placement means no equipment purchase and no installation charge, and the machine remains part of the service. That result is not guaranteed for every enquiry. The hire cost guide and the cost comparison describe the shape of the cost — the machine, the stock and the staff time — without a price list.

Which office setup should we ask for?

A combination machine is the usual first question for a single break area with mixed snack and drink demand. Separate cabinets suit higher volume. Coffee is a separate question from packaged food. Cashless payments suit staff who do not carry coins. Where monitoring is fitted, stock and sales can help plan visits. It is not on every machine.

What does servicing include?

On a machine in the managed service, servicing can include installation, replenishment, product changes, routine maintenance and technical support after a fault is reported through the agreed contact. Refill timing follows usage, capacity, opening hours and access. No response time is published. Where monitoring is fitted, it can show stock, sales or machine status. It is not on every machine. Cleaning requirements are agreed for the chosen setup.
