What an office and workplace vending service includes

For a qualifying location there is no equipment purchase and no installation charge. The equipment remains part of the managed vending service. VendingNI looks after stocking and routine support, so the host organisation does not normally manage day-to-day stocking.

The work can include machine supply and installation, an initial product mix, planned replenishment, product changes, routine servicing, maintenance and machine support. Refill frequency follows usage, footfall, the operating pattern, machine capacity, opening hours and delivery access.

Office vending can cover the working day, including outside café or catering hours, where people would otherwise leave the building. The range can include confectionery, crisps, cold drinks and healthier choices, and it can change as demand changes.

An experienced partner network can provide installation, stocking, refills, maintenance and technical support, with cashless payments and monitoring on suitable equipment. What is included depends on the site and the agreed setup. See free managed vending, the managed vending service and the partner network.

Routine servicing and machine support are included for machines in the managed service. Faults are reported through the agreed support contact. They can involve payment systems, product delivery, refrigeration, configuration or general wear, and no fixed response time applies to every site. See vending machine repairs and servicing.

Which workplaces it suits

Offices

A break area, reception or staff room where employees and visitors need snacks and drinks without a staffed shop. Place the machine where it is visible, accessible and practical to replenish.

Hybrid offices

Hybrid attendance, break patterns and visitor traffic can make the real daily population more important than total headcount. Kitchen limitations also matter when the only on-site option is a small staff kitchen.

Factories and warehouses

Shift-based sites often need refreshments outside canteen hours. Assessment looks at workforce by shift, break timing and access. See factory and warehouse vending.

Other workplaces with regular demand include gyms, hotels and hospitals. Coworking stays on its own page: vending for shared working spaces.

Machine options for offices

Different workplaces need different products. The range can include snack vending machines for crisps, chocolate and other snacks, cold drinks vending machines, combination machines that bring snacks and chilled drinks together in one break-area position, and coffee vending machines where there is recurring hot-drink demand.

Healthier snack and drink choices can be included where they suit the people using the machine.

VendingNI supplies modern SandenVendo vending equipment as part of the service range. Machine size and configuration are selected around available space, expected usage, product requirements and the needs of the location. The SandenVendo G-Snack overview explains one snack and combination format. Compare formats on the vending machines page.

Cashless payments

Compatible machines can accept debit cards, contactless payments, Apple Pay and Google Pay. Available methods depend on the selected machine and payment reader. Cashless payment is convenient in an office where staff do not carry coins. Cash acceptance may also be available on some equipment. Not every method is fitted to every machine.

Where suitable monitoring is fitted, stock, sales and machine-status information can help plan visits and show which products are selling. See cashless vending for card and mobile-wallet payments.

How qualification and cost work

For a qualifying location there is no equipment purchase and no installation charge. VendingNI does not publish prices or a hire tariff. The cost comparison sets managed vending next to buying and renting.

Approximately 40 regular workplace users is a typical starting guideline, not an acceptance rule. The qualification guide sets out how staff numbers, footfall, space, access, products, hybrid attendance and break patterns are assessed. A smaller workplace may still qualify where customer footfall, long opening hours or shift demand creates consistent usage.
