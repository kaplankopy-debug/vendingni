Coworking Vending Machines, Fully Managed

Coworking vending machines for members, tenants and visitors, looked after as a fully managed service so the workspace team does not take on another daily task.

Shared-workspace machines

No coworking model is published with a cups-per-day figure. The usual request is a cashless combination machine, with coffee assessed separately if the shared kitchen is limited.

Combination cabinet

A combination machine can cover mixed snack and drink demand in one footprint. The Sanden Vendo G-Snack is the named example.

Coffee

Where the shared kitchen does not cover hot drinks, coffee vending or a bean-to-cup office machine can be assessed. No cups-per-day rating is published for either.

SandenVendo and cashless

VendingNI supplies SandenVendo equipment. Size and configuration follow the space, the expected usage and the products. No cups-per-day rating is published for it. Cashless payments suit members from different companies who will not share a cash tin.

How a shared workspace gets the service

The managed service lets the product range and the refill schedule follow actual use. The workspace team does not buy the stock.

  • The machine is supplied and installed once a placement is agreed
  • The first fill, later replenishment and product changes are handled for the site
  • Routine servicing, maintenance and technical support sit with the managed service
  • Card, contactless, Apple Pay and Google Pay depend on the machine and the reader

A single-company office is a different enquiry. Use office and workplace vending when the site is one employer rather than a shared workspace.

Which shared workspaces it suits, by size

Daily attendance

Registered membership is a weak guide. Assessment uses typical daily attendance, day users, visitors and events. About 40 people in on a normal day is the workplace-style starting point, not a rule.

Changing occupancy

Shared buildings can be quiet one day and full the next. The busy pattern, not the name on the door, decides whether replenishment is practical.

Communal placement

A visible shared position supports access across flexible hours, including times when a staffed café would be closed.

Questions people ask

What does it cost?

There is no tariff on this page. For a qualifying location, a fully managed placement means no equipment purchase and no installation charge, and the machine remains part of the service. That result is not guaranteed for every enquiry. The hire cost guide and the cost comparison describe the shape of the cost — the machine, the stock and the staff time — without a price list.

Which shared-workspace setup should we ask for?

A cashless combination machine is the usual first request. Add coffee if hot drinks are the gap. Send typical occupancy, visitor traffic, opening hours, event use, products and the proposed position.

What does servicing include?

On a machine in the managed service, servicing can include installation, replenishment, product changes, routine maintenance and technical support after a fault is reported through the agreed contact. Refill timing follows usage, capacity, opening hours and access. No response time is published. Where monitoring is fitted, it can show stock, sales or machine status. It is not on every machine. Cleaning requirements are agreed for the chosen setup.

Tell us about the workspace

Email the location, how many people are typically in, the opening hours, and whether members need snacks, cold drinks, coffee, or all three.
