What the Northern Ireland service includes

A fully managed placement can include supplying and setting up the machine, an initial product mix, replenishment, routine servicing and technical support when a fault is reported. The host organisation does not normally manage day-to-day stocking.

  • The machine is supplied and installed once a placement is agreed
  • The first fill, later replenishment and product changes are handled for the site
  • Routine servicing, maintenance and technical support sit with the managed service
  • Card, contactless, Apple Pay and Google Pay depend on the machine and the reader

Who it suits, by site size and by town

About 40 regular users is a typical workplace starting point, subject to assessment. Smaller workplaces may still qualify where footfall, long opening hours or shift demand creates consistent usage. Hotels are often discussed from about 50 rooms and apartment buildings from about 200 apartments.

Below the usual workplace guideline

About 40 regular workplace users is a starting point, not a rule. A smaller site can still be assessed when footfall, long opening hours or shift demand creates steady use. Hybrid attendance means the number of people actually in the building matters more than the payroll.

Around 40 regular users

That is the typical workplace guideline used across this site. It is an assessment starting point. Space, delivery access, products and break patterns are still part of the decision.

Larger workplaces

An office of about 80 people is used on the machines page as a selection example, not a promise of acceptance. A combination machine may cover snacks and chilled drinks in one break area, with coffee assessed separately if hot drinks are the real demand.

Enquiries are assessed across Northern Ireland, not from a list of towns with their own pages. Belfast, Antrim, Lisburn, Dungannon and Mallusk already come up in commercial enquiries, and so do other towns. What changes from place to place is the kind of site, not the service. The factors stay the same: staff numbers, visitor footfall, space, delivery access, preferred products, usage, hybrid attendance and break patterns.

Belfast

Belfast is where city-centre offices, hospitals, hotels and visitor venues sit close together. A machine in a reception or a staff corridor has to be reachable for deliveries without blocking the working day, and footfall can come from visitors as much as from employees. The dedicated page is vending machines in Belfast.

Antrim and Newtownabbey

Antrim and Newtownabbey are assessed as workplaces and industrial sites. Where the building runs shifts, the break pattern matters more than a single lunch hour. The assessment needs that pattern, plus space and a practical place for a delivery.

Lisburn

In Lisburn, the sites to describe are offices, schools and other workplaces where people come and go through the day. The number of people on site can differ from the number attached to the address, especially where staff are in for only part of the week. A school or college is judged on who is actually in the building, and on that site’s rules for products and access.

Mallusk

In Mallusk, factories, warehouses and other workplaces are assessed on whether a machine can be replenished around shifts. Delivery access, where the machine would stand, and when people can use it matter more than a reception drinks point. Factory and warehouse vending sets out that kind of site.

Dungannon

Dungannon is assessed on the same factors as anywhere else in Northern Ireland: who uses the building, how long it is open, whether a machine can stand somewhere visible, and whether products can be delivered and replenished. Snack, cold drink, combination and coffee machines are considered on the same basis, with healthier options where they suit the people using the machine.

Other towns

Offices, factories, hotels, gyms, hospitals, schools and care settings elsewhere in Northern Ireland are assessed in the same way. For sites in England, Scotland or Wales, see fully managed vending across the UK.

Questions people ask

What does it cost?

There is no tariff on this page. For a qualifying location, a fully managed placement means no equipment purchase and no installation charge, and the machine remains part of the service. That result is not guaranteed for every enquiry. The hire cost guide and the cost comparison describe the shape of the cost — the machine, the stock and the staff time — without a price list.

Which Northern Ireland setup should we ask for?

Offices, factories, warehouses, hotels, gyms, care settings and other venues with regular demand can enquire. Postcode alone is not a promise. Say the town, how many people use the site, the hours, the products and where a machine could stand.

What does servicing include?

On a machine in the managed service, servicing can include installation, replenishment, product changes, routine maintenance and technical support after a fault is reported through the agreed contact. Refill timing follows usage, capacity, opening hours and access. No response time is published. Where monitoring is fitted, it can show stock, sales or machine status. It is not on every machine. Cleaning requirements are agreed for the chosen setup.

Do you provide vending machine service across Northern Ireland?

Yes. VendingNI assesses qualifying workplaces and venues across Northern Ireland, including Belfast, Antrim, Lisburn, Dungannon, Mallusk and other towns, subject to site suitability and service logistics.

Can a smaller workplace qualify?

Sometimes. Around 40 regular workplace users is an indicative starting point, but strong footfall, long opening hours or shift demand can make a smaller location suitable.
