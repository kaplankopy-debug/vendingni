A vending machine company is usually asked to provide the machine, keep it stocked and deal with faults. That is the service here. For a qualifying location there is no equipment purchase and no installation charge. The machine stays part of the managed service, and the host organisation does not normally run day-to-day stocking. Service delivery is supported through an established vending network, with experienced partners. If you are in the home region, start with vending machines in Northern Ireland.

How fully managed vending works across the UK

The route is the same wherever the site sits in the UK.

Tell us about your site

Send the location, the type of workplace or venue, and roughly how many people use it.

We assess suitability

Staff numbers, visitor footfall, space, delivery access, preferred products, usage, hybrid attendance and break patterns are all part of the assessment. About 40 regular users is a typical starting guideline, subject to assessment.

Installation

Once a placement is agreed, the machine is supplied and installed, then prepared with an initial product mix. The configuration is chosen around the space and the products people are likely to buy.

We manage it

Planned replenishment, product changes, routine servicing, maintenance and machine support continue under the managed service. The organisation using the machine does not have to arrange those visits itself.

A placement is not guaranteed for every enquiry. Low usage, awkward access or a position that cannot be replenished safely can mean a site is not suitable, even when the headcount looks promising on paper.

What the service includes

A fully managed arrangement is built around the work that keeps a machine useful after it is installed, not only the day it arrives.

Supply and installation cover selecting a machine, delivering it and setting it up. Stocking covers the first fill, then planned replenishment and product changes. Servicing covers routine maintenance and machine support when a fault is reported through the agreed contact.

Cashless and contactless payments can be fitted on compatible equipment: debit cards, Apple Pay and Google Pay, depending on the machine and payment reader. Cash acceptance may also be available on some equipment. Monitoring, where it is fitted, can show stock, sales and machine status so refill visits can be planned. It is not on every installation. Healthier snack and drink choices can be included where they suit the people using the machine.

Vending machines we provide

The range covers snack machines, cold drink machines, combination machines and coffee machines. Snack machines suit crisps, chocolate and other packaged snacks. Cold drink machines suit chilled drinks. A combination machine brings snacks and chilled drinks together where a break area has room for only one unit. Coffee machines cover hot-drink demand through the working day.

For a self-service hot-drinks cabinet, see coffee vending machines across the UK. For a bean-to-cup machine in an office, including rental and lease, see office coffee machines. Formats are compared on the vending machines page. VendingNI supplies SandenVendo equipment, with size and configuration selected around space, expected usage and the products the site needs. That choice is part of the assessment.

Hire, rental or fully managed?

Hire, rental and lease usually mean paying to use equipment for a period. Stocking and servicing may sit with the site, or they may be a separate arrangement. A fully managed service is different. For a qualifying location there is no equipment purchase and no installation charge. The machine is supplied, installed, replenished and serviced as one arrangement, and it remains part of that service.

Who provides the machine, who stocks it and who services it is set out on vending machine hire across the UK.

Who it suits

The service is for organisations with regular demand. Each site type below has its own page because the practical questions differ.

  • Office vending machines for offices and other workplaces, including hybrid attendance and break patterns that change the real daily population.
  • Factories and warehouses, where shifts and canteen hours often decide whether a machine is used.
  • Schools and colleges, where access, site rules and the people using the machine shape the product range.
  • Hospitals, for staff, patients and visitors who need refreshments outside a staffed outlet.

  • Shared workspaces, where members, tenants and visitors share one refreshment point.

Is my site suitable?

The factors are staff numbers, visitor footfall, space, delivery access, preferred products, usage, hybrid attendance and break patterns. A large payroll with few people on site can be less suitable than a smaller workplace with steady visitors or long shifts. About 40 regular users is a typical starting guideline, subject to assessment. It is not a hard minimum, and it is not an acceptance rule. A smaller site may still be considered where footfall, opening hours or shifts create consistent usage.
