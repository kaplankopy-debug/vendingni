Hire, rental and lease are not the same contract, even though the words are often used as if they were. This page sets out what those models usually involve, then sets them next to the fully managed service. For the wider managed service, read fully managed vending across the UK.

What vending machine hire, rental and leasing usually involve

In general, vending machine hire means paying to have a machine on site for a period without owning it at the end. Vending machine rental is often the same idea: use of equipment for a term, rather than a purchase. A vending machine lease is commonly a finance arrangement over an agreed term. The labels overlap. This page does not describe any particular provider's contract.

On a typical hire or rental, someone still has to fill the machine, choose the products and arrange help when it fails. That work may be included, or it may be a second agreement. On a lease, the site often still carries the operating work. Those models are a poor fit when the organisation wanted the refreshments looked after, and instead received a machine it now has to run.

Buying is the other end. The organisation pays for the equipment, then arranges delivery, installation, stock and repairs, or pays others to do each job. How much a vending machine costs explains the kinds of cost that come with ownership. It is not a hire tariff for this service.

When a fully managed service makes more sense

A managed placement is the better question when nobody on site wants to own the stock or arrange repairs. Offices, factories whose canteen closes between shifts, and hotels with demand outside restaurant hours are typical. The common thread is recurring use and a preference not to run the machine.

It is the wrong question if the organisation wants to own the asset and choose products from its own supplier. It is also the wrong question where usage is too low to support replenishment, the only position is impractical, or delivery access would make routine visits unrealistic. Where a managed service is the right question, the steps are: tell us about your site, we assess suitability, the machine is installed once agreed, and we then manage replenishment and routine support.

FAQs

Can I hire or rent a vending machine in the UK?

You can enquire about vending machine hire, rental or lease, or about a fully managed service. For a qualifying location, VendingNI supplies the machine with no equipment purchase and no installation charge, then looks after stocking and routine support. Each site is assessed.

What usually sits with the site on a hire, rental or lease?

Hire, rental and lease usually mean paying to use equipment for an agreed period. Stocking and servicing are often the site's responsibility, or they are arranged separately. The detail varies by provider, so the table on this page is a general comparison, not a tariff.

When is a fully managed service the better fit?

It fits when the organisation wants the machine, the products and the routine support handled together, and the location qualifies. There is no equipment purchase and no installation charge. Replenishment, product changes, servicing, maintenance and machine support are part of the managed service.

Which machines can be supplied?

Snack, cold drink, combination and coffee machines. Healthier options can be included where they suit the people using the machine. VendingNI supplies SandenVendo equipment. Cashless payments, including debit cards, Apple Pay and Google Pay, depend on the selected machine and payment reader.

How is a UK site assessed for a managed placement?

We look at staff numbers, visitor footfall, space, delivery access, preferred products, usage, hybrid attendance and break patterns. About 40 regular users is a typical starting guideline, subject to assessment. A placement is not guaranteed from headcount alone.
