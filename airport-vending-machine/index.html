Airport and transport vending

Vending Machines for Airports & Transport Locations

Snacks and drinks for airports and other transport locations, available beyond staffed retail hours. Qualifying sites are supplied as a fully managed service.

Transport machines, sized to footfall rather than a published rating

No airport model is published with a vends-per-day figure. High-use locations may need larger capacity or more than one machine. The named equipment family is SandenVendo.

Snack, drinks, or both

Grab-and-go demand can use a snack, drinks or combination machine. More than one cabinet may be needed where traffic is high.

SandenVendo equipment

VendingNI supplies SandenVendo equipment. Size and configuration follow the space, the expected usage and the products. No cups-per-day rating is published for it. The Sanden Vendo G-Snack is the named snack and combination example. The overview describes the format. It does not publish a daily vend figure.

Traveller payments

Card and mobile-wallet payments suit people who are not carrying local cash. The reader still has to match the machine.

How a transport site gets the service

High-use sites may need frequent replenishment. Compatible monitoring can help plan those visits and is not guaranteed on every machine.

  • The machine is supplied and installed once a placement is agreed
  • The first fill, later replenishment and product changes are handled for the site
  • Routine servicing, maintenance and technical support sit with the managed service
  • Card, contactless, Apple Pay and Google Pay depend on the machine and the reader

The machine supplements existing retail. It does not replace a shop the site already runs. See high-footfall network operating experience. Secure access and any service restrictions on the site are part of the assessment.

Which transport sites it suits, by size of use

Passenger flow

There is no published passenger threshold. Assessment uses footfall, dwell time, how long people are in the area, and whether that flow is steady enough to support replenishment.

Long hours

Airports and stations often need refreshments outside staffed retail hours. The operating pattern matters as much as the busiest hour.

Placement and access

The machine should follow passenger routes without blocking circulation, and replenishment has to be allowed under the site’s access rules.

Questions people ask

What does it cost?

There is no tariff on this page. For a qualifying location, a fully managed placement means no equipment purchase and no installation charge, and the machine remains part of the service. That result is not guaranteed for every enquiry. The hire cost guide and the cost comparison describe the shape of the cost — the machine, the stock and the staff time — without a price list.

Which transport setup should we ask for?

High-capacity snack, drinks or combination vending can support grab-and-go demand. Say the footfall, the hours, the access constraints, the proposed position and the products.

What does servicing include?

On a machine in the managed service, servicing can include installation, replenishment, product changes, routine maintenance and technical support after a fault is reported through the agreed contact. Refill timing follows usage, capacity, opening hours and access. No response time is published. Where monitoring is fitted, it can show stock, sales or machine status. It is not on every machine. Cleaning requirements are agreed for the chosen setup.
