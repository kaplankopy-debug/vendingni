How to Choose an Office Coffee Machine

A practical way to choose the format and the arrangement before you enquire. It compares bean-to-cup with a hot drinks cabinet, and rental or lease with a fully managed machine. The service is office coffee machines. A self-service cabinet is coffee vending machines.

Bean-to-cup, or another format

Start with who the drinks are for and who will look after the machine. The format follows from that. Say the break you have, and the machine is chosen for that room.

  • Bean-to-cup. The machine grinds beans for the drink. It suits a staff room, reception or smaller office where people want coffee from beans rather than a queue at a cabinet. Managed bean-to-cup office coffee is something VendingNI supplies. The drinks depend on the selected equipment and ingredient system. The menu is agreed for the machine chosen for your site.
  • Hot drinks cabinet. A coffee vending machine is a self-service cabinet. People serve themselves, including at a shift change or a large break. On VendingNI's UK coffee page, a hot drinks machine is that same managed coffee vending: coffee and any other hot drink agreed for the equipment. Read coffee vending and hot drinks machines if that is the break you have.
  • Filter jug, capsule or instant. Offices often compare those as well. VendingNI supplies managed bean-to-cup machines and managed hot-drinks cabinets. The useful question is who fills the water, who buys the ingredients, and who cleans the machine. If nobody on site will do that, a managed machine is the relevant question.

A team room and a factory floor are not the same brief. Bean-to-cup is the usual office question. A cabinet is the usual question when many people arrive in a short window. Say which one you have. Do not ask for both unless both breaks exist.

Rental, lease, or fully managed

These are three different questions. Mixing them up is how an enquiry comes back unclear. Rental and lease terms depend on the site and the machine; get in touch and we'll talk you through the options.

  • Coffee machine rental. The business uses a machine without buying it. People also say hire for the same idea. What a rental includes, beyond use of the machine, depends on the site and the machine. Rental is not guaranteed for every enquiry, and it is not the free option.
  • Coffee machine lease. This is also use of a machine without buying it. VendingNI offers lease as well as rental. Say which word you mean, and whether anyone on site will restock and clean the machine. A lease is not guaranteed either.
  • Fully managed. For a qualifying workplace there is no equipment purchase and no installation charge. The machine remains part of the service. Restocking and servicing are included for the machines VendingNI manages. The organisation does not normally run day-to-day restocking. That result is not guaranteed. A workplace that does not qualify can still ask about rental or lease. Only the managed arrangement is free, and only where the workplace qualifies.

If the real aim is that nobody on the team buys stock or calls an engineer, say that. That is the managed question, described as fully managed vending. If the aim is to have the machine without owning it, and the site may not qualify for a free placement, say rental or lease. The wider comparison with vending hire is on vending machine hire across the UK. How a free placement is assessed is on how to get a free vending machine.

What to ask before you enquire

You do not need a specification. You need a short brief the assessment can use. These are the questions worth answering in the email, in ordinary language.

  • How many people are in the building on a normal day? If attendance is hybrid, count people actually in, not the number on the payroll.
  • What do they drink now, and is the busy moment a few rounds in a team room or a queue at a shift change?
  • Is the question rental, lease, or the fully managed option? If you do not know, say whether anyone on site will restock and clean.
  • Where can the machine stand, and can someone reach it to replenish ingredients during the hours the building is open?
  • Is there already a rule about what can be served? Some workplaces have one. Write it down rather than leaving it implied.

The same brief works for an office and for a team room in a factory or warehouse when the demand is coffee for that team. Snack and cold-drink machines are a separate choice, on office vending machines.

Space, water, power and usage

Describe the room: the power point, the water and the space to replenish ingredients. The useful note is what is already there.

  • Power. Say whether there is a power point where the machine would stand, or whether one would have to be added. Do not assume a machine can share a socket with a kettle and a microwave.
  • Water. Ask, when you enquire, whether the machine you are discussing needs a plumbed water supply or a tank that someone fills, and who fills a tank. Say which of those the room already has.
  • Space. Floor or counter space has to include room to replenish ingredients without blocking a walkway or a fire route. Delivery access matters on the day it arrives, not only the footprint once it is in place.
  • Usage. About 40 regular users is a typical starting guideline on this site, not a pass mark. Expected cups and the peak can matter as much as headcount. A smaller team that each take several hot drinks can be a clearer case than a large payroll that brings coffee from home. Expected cups are your description of current demand. They are not a machine rating.

If you cannot answer water and power yet, say so. An assessment can still start from headcount, the break pattern and the room you have in mind. Capacity is planned from how many people use the machine and when they take breaks.

What is agreed for your site

Brands, models, cups per day, contract lengths and prices are agreed for the site and the machine. Rental and lease terms depend on the site and the machine. A workplace is assessed before any placement. Send the brief, and the format is chosen from that.
