How Does Vending Machine Hire Work?

How vending machine hire works depends on which arrangement you are being offered: a fully managed service, hire or rental of the equipment, or a purchase. The useful difference is who supplies the machine, who keeps it stocked, and who attends when it faults. The same questions apply to workplaces and venues in England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.

Three ways to get a vending machine on site

There are three ordinary ways to get a machine on site.

A fully managed service means the operator supplies, installs, stocks and services the machine. For a qualifying site there is no equipment purchase and no installation charge. The machine stays part of the service. People in the building buy from the machine.

Hire or rental means a machine on site without buying it. What is included, and how long the agreement runs, depend on the agreement. Stock, cleaning and faults may sit with the site, or they may be included.

Buying means the organisation owns the machine. Stocking, cleaning and repairs then sit with the organisation, or with someone else appointed to do them.

VendingNI's route is the fully managed service, described as fully managed vending. For a workplace, about 40 regular users is a typical starting guideline, subject to assessment. Every site is assessed before a machine is placed. Snack, drinks, combination and coffee are set out on the machines page. A dedicated healthy vending range is available, and the range is agreed with the site. Office coffee machines can also be rented or leased, with no terms set out here. See office coffee machines.

How vending machine hire works, step by step

A fully managed placement follows six steps.

  1. Tell us about the site: the location, roughly how many people use it, where the machine would go, and power.
  2. The site is assessed on use, space, access, hours and the products wanted.
  3. The format and the range are agreed: snack, drinks, combination or coffee. A dedicated healthy vending range is available where the site wants it.
  4. The machine is installed.
  5. It is stocked and serviced. On a managed machine the site does not normally buy the stock. Refill timing follows usage, capacity, opening hours and access.
  6. Faults are reported through the agreed contact.

Payment is cashless where that is agreed, as stated elsewhere on the site. Whether a card reader is fitted is confirmed for that machine.

If the aim is a qualifying fully managed machine, rather than equipment the site will run, read how to get a free vending machine.

Who pays for a hired vending machine?

On the fully managed model, a qualifying site pays nothing for the machine or the installation. People buy from the machine. The organisation does not purchase the cabinet and does not pay an installation charge.

Under hire or rental, the organisation pays to use a machine it does not own. Equipment, delivery, stock, refills and servicing can each be included or left with the site. There is no single figure. What moves the cost is explained in the vending machine hire cost guide.

Under buying, the organisation pays for the machine and then for stocking, cleaning and repairs. The same questions apply in England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.

What your site needs

A machine needs floor space, a way to deliver and refill it, and power. Say where it would go, and whether a socket is there. Do not assume it can share a socket with a kettle and a microwave. Water applies to bean-to-cup coffee, not to a snack or cold drinks cabinet. Say if water is nearby, and who would fill a tank.

Space, a mains socket and access are set out in what your workplace needs to host a vending machine.

Hiring a vending machine in the UK and Northern Ireland

England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland are assessed on the same basis: the people on site, the hours, the space, and whether the position can be replenished.

See vending machine hire across the UK for hire, rental and lease beside a fully managed machine. For a site in Northern Ireland, see vending machine hire in Northern Ireland. Those pages cover the offer in that place. This guide covers how the arrangement works.

Say whether you want a managed service, a hire you will run, or a machine you will own. Include the location, roughly how many people use the site, and whether you need snacks, drinks, a combination cabinet or coffee.

Questions about how hire works

Can you rent a vending machine?

Yes. Hire or rental puts a machine on site without buying it. What is included, and how long the agreement runs, depend on the agreement. For a qualifying site, VendingNI's route is a fully managed service, with no equipment purchase and no installation charge.

Do companies pay for vending machines?

On a fully managed placement, a qualifying site pays nothing for the machine or the installation. People buy from the machine. Under hire or rental, a company pays to use equipment it does not own. Under buying, the company pays for the machine and then for stocking, cleaning and repairs.

Do you rent or buy a vending machine?

Rent if you want a machine without owning it. Buy if you want to own the equipment and take on stocking, cleaning and repairs. A qualifying site can instead take a fully managed service, where the operator supplies, stocks and services the machine. About 40 regular users is a typical starting guideline, subject to assessment.

Who stocks a hired vending machine?

It depends on the agreement. On hire or rental, stocking is often left with the site unless stocking is included. On a fully managed placement, stocking and servicing sit with the service. Faults on a managed machine are reported through the agreed contact.
