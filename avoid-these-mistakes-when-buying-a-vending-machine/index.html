Common Mistakes to Avoid When Buying a Vending Machine

Buying vending equipment can make sense in some situations, but the wrong machine or location can turn an apparently simple purchase into an ongoing operational problem.

1. Buying before understanding demand

A machine should be sized around realistic usage. Too much capacity can tie up money and stock, while too little capacity can cause frequent sell-outs.

2. Ignoring access and positioning

Check doorways, lifts, floor loading, power access and the final machine position before delivery. Vending equipment is large and heavy, so access should be considered early.

3. Treating cashless payment as an afterthought

Many users now expect to pay by card or mobile wallet. Confirm compatibility before buying older equipment.

4. Underestimating stock management

Someone needs to choose products, buy stock, rotate dates, refill the machine and respond when demand changes.

5. Forgetting maintenance

Refrigeration, payment hardware, motors and delivery mechanisms may all require servicing over the life of the machine.

6. Buying when a managed service would be simpler

If the purpose is simply to provide refreshments for staff or visitors, ownership may not be necessary. Qualifying sites can consider a fully managed vending service instead.
