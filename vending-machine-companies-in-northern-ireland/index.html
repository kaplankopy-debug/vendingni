Choosing a vending provider

Vending Machine Companies in Northern Ireland

A comparison page for organisations choosing a vending machine company in Northern Ireland. VendingNI’s offer is a fully managed service for qualifying workplaces and venues.

What to ask a supplier to name

A useful supplier can say which machine format fits the site. VendingNI names SandenVendo equipment and the G-Snack example. It does not publish a cups-per-day table or a competitor scorecard.

SandenVendo equipment

VendingNI supplies SandenVendo equipment. Size and configuration follow the space, the expected usage and the products. No cups-per-day rating is published for it.

Sanden Vendo G-Snack

The Sanden Vendo G-Snack is the named snack and combination example. The overview describes the format. It does not publish a daily vend figure.

Formats to compare

Look at snack, drinks, combination, coffee and cashless before you enquire. A useful provider matches capacity and configuration to space and demand, rather than offering one standard machine to every site.

Managed service, hire, or buying

Most organisations want a refreshment service, not another job for their staff. Buying gives ownership and usually leaves stock, payments, maintenance and repairs with your team. Hire or rental can reduce the upfront equipment cost, although responsibilities still vary. VendingNI’s usual model is a fully managed service for qualifying organisations: the site does not purchase the machine, and stocking and routine support are managed after installation.

No hire tariff is published here. The comparison that matters is who does the work after installation, and whether the site is likely to qualify. Confirm that refill and support visits can reach the location. Coverage is discussed on the Northern Ireland service page and, for the city, Belfast.

Workplace sites can start with office and workplace vending. Sector pages are listed under vending solutions. The free managed vending guide explains qualification. The contact page is there if you want to send details without choosing a path yet.

Which sites are worth an enquiry, by size

Use the same size tests you would use with any supplier. About 40 regular workplace users is VendingNI’s published starting guideline. It is not an acceptance rule, and it is not a score against other companies.

Below the usual workplace guideline

About 40 regular workplace users is a starting point, not a rule. A smaller site can still be assessed when footfall, long opening hours or shift demand creates steady use. Hybrid attendance means the number of people actually in the building matters more than the payroll.

Around 40 regular users

That is the typical workplace guideline used across this site. It is an assessment starting point. Space, delivery access, products and break patterns are still part of the decision.

Larger workplaces

An office of about 80 people is used on the machines page as a selection example, not a promise of acceptance. A combination machine may cover snacks and chilled drinks in one break area, with coffee assessed separately if hot drinks are the real demand.

Hotels from about 50 rooms and apartment buildings from about 200 apartments are the other published guidelines. A company that will not ask for users, hours, space and access is not yet assessing the site.

Questions people ask

What does it cost?

There is no tariff on this page. For a qualifying location, a fully managed placement means no equipment purchase and no installation charge, and the machine remains part of the service. That result is not guaranteed for every enquiry. The hire cost guide and the cost comparison describe the shape of the cost — the machine, the stock and the staff time — without a price list.

Which question should we put to a vending company?

Ask who fills the machine, who attends a fault, and whether you are being offered a managed service, a hire agreement or a sale. Then ask how the machine will be matched to your space, users and products. If you already want the managed route, send the location and approximate usage.

What does servicing include?

On a machine in the managed service, servicing can include installation, replenishment, product changes, routine maintenance and technical support after a fault is reported through the agreed contact. Refill timing follows usage, capacity, opening hours and access. No response time is published. Where monitoring is fitted, it can show stock, sales or machine status. It is not on every machine. Cleaning requirements are agreed for the chosen setup.
