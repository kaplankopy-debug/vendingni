Choosing a Healthy Workplace Vending Mix

A practical set of questions for an employer deciding how a healthier selection should sit in a workplace machine. It does not list products. The range itself is agreed with the site, and the managed service is described on healthy vending machines.

Questions to ask before you enquire

Start with the people who will actually use the machine, not with a product catalogue. VendingNI does not publish a brand list, a nutrition standard or a certification for a healthy range. A dedicated healthy vending range is available, and the useful brief is what this site will allow and who is buying.

  • Who uses the machine: staff only, visitors as well, or a wider public such as gym members, pupils or patients’ visitors?
  • What do they already buy when they have a choice: packaged snacks, cold drinks, hot drinks, or some of each?
  • Does the organisation already restrict what can be sold? Schools, hospitals and care homes often do. Write that rule down before asking for a range.
  • Is the healthier selection meant to be part of the cabinet, or the whole cabinet?
  • Where can the machine stand, and can it be replenished during the hours the building is open?

An office brief is not the same as a school brief. Office vending machines are judged on the people in the building. A school or hospital should send its own access and product rules with the enquiry.

How to balance demand

People in the same building rarely want one type of product. A mix that removes every familiar snack can disappoint the people who were using the machine for those lines. A mix that only adds a token healthier item can disappoint the people who asked for the change. Neither outcome is a published sales figure. It is a reason to describe demand honestly.

A workable brief usually says one of three things:

  • Mixed cabinet. Keep a standard snack and drinks selection, and add healthier lines where they suit the people using the machine. Snack and drinks pages describe those standard formats.
  • Dedicated range. Ask for the healthy range in place of the usual mix, and say so plainly. That is a site decision, not a default.
  • Drinks-led or snack-led. If the real demand is water and cold drinks, or packaged food, say which one matters more. The other can still be included. It should not be guessed.

You do not need a forecast. Say what people ask for now, what the organisation wants to change, and any line it will not allow. The range can be reviewed once it is clear what sells. That review is part of stocking, not a promise about which products will be popular.

How this works under a managed service

On a qualifying site, a fully managed placement means no equipment purchase and no installation charge. The machine remains part of the service. The organisation does not buy the stock or employ someone to fill the cabinet. For a location that does not qualify, that result is not guaranteed.

What changes for the mix, compared with buying a machine and filling it yourself:

  • You agree the range. You do not pick from a public price list or a published product menu.
  • The first fill, later replenishment and product changes are handled for the site.
  • A line that sells slowly can be changed. You do not have to eat the stock yourself to find that out.
  • Card, contactless, Apple Pay and Google Pay depend on the machine and the reader. They are not a separate healthy-range feature.

Refill timing follows usage, capacity, opening hours and access. No response time is published. The service model, including who it is for, is the fully managed vending service. Formats other than a healthy range are on the machines page.

What this guide does not claim

It does not name products, brands, calories, allergens or nutrition standards. It does not say that a healthier mix is required, or that it will change what people eat. It does not give a price, a service level or a fleet size. Those facts are not published.

Coverage for the managed service is across the UK (England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland), with service coverage in the Republic of Ireland. Whether a particular building qualifies is a site assessment, not a conclusion this guide can reach. If you want the placement itself, use healthy vending machines and send the brief from the questions above.

Send the brief with the enquiry

Email the location, who uses the site, roughly how many people are in on a normal day, and whether you want a mixed cabinet or a dedicated range. Include any product rule the organisation already has.
