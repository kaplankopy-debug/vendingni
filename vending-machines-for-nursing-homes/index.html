Care home vending

Nursing and Care Home Vending Machines

Vending machines for nursing and care homes, for staff, visitors and suitable shared areas. Stocking is a fully managed service where the home qualifies.

Care-home machines, agreed for the people who will use them

No care-home model is published with a cups-per-day figure, and no clinical or dietary claim is made. The choice is a drinks or combination cabinet in a place the home is willing to use.

Combination or drinks

A combination machine or a drinks machine may suit an accessible communal or staff area. The Sanden Vendo G-Snack is the named snack and combination example.

SandenVendo equipment

VendingNI supplies SandenVendo equipment. Size and configuration follow the space, the expected usage and the products. No cups-per-day rating is published for it.

Payments and controls

Payment methods and the product range are agreed for the users and the home’s own rules. Cashless payments depend on the reader. No clinical suitability is assumed.

How a care home gets the service

For a qualifying home, equipment, replenishment, routine servicing and support are arranged through the vending network. The care team does not run a vending operation.

  • The machine is supplied and installed once a placement is agreed
  • The first fill, later replenishment and product changes are handled for the site
  • Routine servicing, maintenance and technical support sit with the managed service
  • Card, contactless, Apple Pay and Google Pay depend on the machine and the reader

Published vending-network stories include care settings. They are described on the network experience page and are not presented as VendingNI-owned case studies. Product choice follows the home’s policy.

Which homes it suits, by size of use

Staff and visitors

There is no published resident-count threshold for care homes. Assessment uses staff numbers, visitor patterns, hours, accessibility, supervision and access for deliveries.

Residents in shared areas

A machine may serve suitable communal space as well as a staff room. Placement and products have to match the people using it and the home’s own rules.

Round the clock

Care settings often need refreshments outside catering hours. That pattern matters more than a single lunchtime headcount.

Questions people ask

What does it cost?

There is no tariff on this page. For a qualifying location, a fully managed placement means no equipment purchase and no installation charge, and the machine remains part of the service. That result is not guaranteed for every enquiry. The hire cost guide and the cost comparison describe the shape of the cost — the machine, the stock and the staff time — without a price list.

Which care-home setup should we ask for?

Describe whether the machine is for a staff room, a reception, or a communal area, and who is allowed to use it. A combination or drinks machine is the usual starting point. Send staff and resident numbers, visitor patterns, hours, accessibility needs and the proposed position.

What does servicing include?

On a machine in the managed service, servicing can include installation, replenishment, product changes, routine maintenance and technical support after a fault is reported through the agreed contact. Refill timing follows usage, capacity, opening hours and access. No response time is published. Where monitoring is fitted, it can show stock, sales or machine status. It is not on every machine. Cleaning requirements are agreed for the chosen setup.
